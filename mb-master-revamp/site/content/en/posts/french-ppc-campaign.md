## Build the campaign for France from the start

Your French campaign is live and the clicks are coming in; the next step is turning them into enquiries. The answer usually sits in the build. A campaign translated from an English one bids on the words English buyers use, shows ads that read like a translation, and lands people on a page built for another market. Rebuilt for France, the same budget pays for clicks from people likely to buy.

The market is worth getting right, and you are competing with advertisers who already write for it natively.

## Research the terms French buyers type

Research is where a French campaign finds its buyers. It gives you the terms a buyer in Lyon actually types, which usually differ from your English campaign's terms in translation. France is also several audiences: buying habits and vocabulary shift from region to region.

Start with your French competitors. Which keywords are they bidding on, and what do their ads promise? Tools like [SEMrush](https://www.semrush.com/) or [SpyFu](https://www.spyfu.com/) show you both, and how their ads perform across regions of France, which points to [insights into potential market](/blog/alternatives-to-google-analytics/) gaps your campaign could fill.

Then look at how your buyers buy. French shoppers favour secure, trusted payment methods, and many are highly sensitive to [pricing](/how-i-work/) transparency. An ad that states the price up front wins that buyer's click.

For [keyword research](/services/technical-seo/), work from French-specific terms and long-tail phrases, researched in French. Google Keyword Planner and the Microsoft Advertising Keyword Planner give you volumes; a native speaker tells you which terms carry buying intent. Watch for regional wording too, since a buyer in Paris may search differently from one in Lyon or Marseille.

Done well, research leaves you with the terms your buyers really use and the gaps your competitors leave.

## Structure campaigns by region on Google Ads and Microsoft Advertising

How you structure the account decides whether you can later tell which region and which offer is paying for itself. Separate campaigns per region give you that answer.

On Google Ads, target French-speaking audiences and use geo-targeting for the regions that matter to you. A Paris campaign usually needs different keywords and messaging from one aimed at southern France, and a separate campaign lets each earn its own budget.

Set French budgets from real click costs in your sector within France. Clicks tend to cost more in competitive areas like Paris, so the budget has to reflect location as well as competition. Visual content often performs well in France, so video is worth a test.

| Attribute | Google Ads | Microsoft Advertising |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach in France | The large majority of searches | A smaller share |
| Competition and CPCs | Higher, especially in Paris | Usually lower |
| Audience | Broad | Skews older and more affluent |
| Extra targeting | Geo and language | Geo, language and LinkedIn profile targeting |

Microsoft Advertising (formerly Bing Ads) is cheaper reach well worth adding to the plan. Its network usually brings lower competition and lower CPCs, and its audience skews older and more affluent, which suits some offers well. Configure geo and language targeting there too. Its LinkedIn profile targeting is particularly useful if you sell B2B in France.

## Write ad copy in native French

Your ad copy is the first sentence a French buyer reads from you, and they notice a translation at once. Native-level French builds trust: it tells the reader you serve their market, and it keeps them on your ad.

Write headlines and descriptions for what a French buyer cares about. Cultural references or [local slang where appropriate to add relevance](/blog/building-a-global-brand/) can make an ad feel written for them, as long as it suits your brand.

Test your calls to action and let the results choose. "Achetez maintenant" (Buy now) works for some audiences; "En savoir plus" (Find out more) often wins where the purchase is considered. The data tells you which one your buyers answer.

## Build landing pages that finish what the ad started

An ad and its landing page work as a pair in PPC: the ad earns the click and the page wins the sale. The landing page has to finish what the ad started, in flawless French and in a context a French buyer recognises.

French buyers look for clear pricing and local testimonials, and a page that shows both earns that trust and converts better. Build for mobile first: French users browse heavily on their phones, so the page must load fast and be easy to use on a small screen.

Align your SEO and PPC work too, so one set of landing pages can rank organically and serve paid traffic, earning twice.

## Set bids and budgets for France

Bidding decides how much each enquiry costs you, which makes it the lever most worth getting right in a competitive market like France.

On Google Ads, a mix of manual and automated bidding usually works best. Manual bidding gives you control over spend; Smart Bidding strategies such as "Target CPA" chase conversions once you have enough data. Adjust bids by region, since some areas need higher bids to compete while others perform well on less.

Microsoft Advertising typically offers lower CPCs than Google, which makes it a cost-effective second channel. Adjust bids by device and location there as well, because French buyers behave differently on mobile and desktop.

Use ad assets (formerly extensions). Sitelinks, callouts and location assets make your ad bigger and more clickable for the same cost per click, and writing them in French keeps the experience consistent.

## Track whether France pays for itself

Market-level tracking tells you whether France is profitable in its own right or subsidised by another market. Set up conversion tracking in Google Analytics for your French campaigns, and watch click-through rate (CTR), cost per click (CPC), conversion rate and return on ad spend (ROAS) for France on its own.

## Three things to watch in French PPC campaigns

Three fixes cover most French campaigns that need a lift, and each one saves money every day it is in place.

The first is regional fit. France is culturally and linguistically varied, so campaigns shaped for each region fit far better than one for the whole country. Adapt your messaging to the areas you target.

The second is native French. Work with native French writers so your ads and landing pages read fluently and naturally, because buyers judge your service by your sentences.

The third is Microsoft Advertising. Its user base is smaller than Google's, and lower competition in France can deliver a strong return on investment.

## Build for France and let the data decide

A French PPC campaign pays when it is built for France: native keyword research, native copy, landing pages a French buyer trusts, and tracking that shows France on its own. Run it on both Google Ads and Microsoft Advertising, then let real data decide where the budget goes.

Keep testing and keep the localization honest, and your French clicks start turning into French customers.
