## Measuring the impact of multilingual SEO on your bottom line

In a global marketplace, website localization and multilingual SEO are core strategies for businesses looking to expand their reach and grow revenue. But how do you measure the return on investment (ROI) of that work?

The post below sets out the metrics and methods for quantifying the impact of website localization and multilingual SEO on your business.

## Understanding website localization and multilingual SEO

Website localization involves adapting your [website content](https://mikebastin.com/optimising-multilingual-website-content/), design and functionality to suit different linguistic and cultural markets. [Multilingual SEO](https://mikebastin.com/services/multilingual-seo/), on the other hand, focuses on optimizing your website for search engines in multiple languages and regions.

Together, these strategies can significantly boost your online presence and revenue in international markets. To justify the investment, you need to measure their impact accurately.

## Key metrics for measuring ROI

Five metrics cover most of what localization and multilingual SEO should change. Track each one per language version, and compare it with your non-localized site or with performance before launch.

| Metric | What it tells you | How to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organic traffic by market | Whether search engines surface the new language versions | Organic sessions by country in [Google Analytics](https://mikebastin.com/alternatives-to-google-analytics/), before and after launch |
| Conversion rate | Whether the content resonates with the audience | Purchases, sign-ups or downloads per localized version |
| Revenue by market | Whether the work pays for itself | E-commerce tracking by country or region |
| Rankings | Whether target keywords win locally | Ahrefs, SEMrush or Moz, per language and region |
| Engagement | Whether the content meets local needs | GA4 engagement rate, average engagement time and pages per session |

Revenue is the metric that settles the ROI question. The others explain why it moved, or why it has not moved yet.

## What the research says about localization and buying

Individual results depend on the market, the product and the quality of the work, so be wary of anyone quoting a guaranteed uplift. The most widely cited consumer research points the same way, though: people buy more readily in their own language.

> 76% of online shoppers prefer to buy products with information in their native language, 40% will never buy from websites in other languages, and 73% want product reviews in their language, if nothing else (survey of 8,709 consumers in 29 countries, November 2019 to January 2020).
>
> Source: [CSA Research, "Can't Read, Won't Buy: B2C", 2020, via Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/survey-of-8-709-consumers-in-29-countries-finds-that-76-prefer-21174283)

The same survey found that 73% of shoppers want product reviews in their language, if nothing else. For an e-commerce site, translated reviews can be a cheap early win. Language is only part of it, and the [cultural differences in multilingual websites](https://mikebastin.com/cultural-differences-in-multilingual-websites/) matter just as much to conversion.

## Building your own benchmarks

ROI varies significantly with industry, target markets and the quality of the work, so published averages are a poor guide to your own case. A better benchmark is your own baseline.

Before a new language goes live, record organic traffic, conversion rate, revenue and customer acquisition cost for that market. Review the same figures at three, six and twelve months, and compare them with a market you have not yet localized.

## Tips for tracking and improving ROI

1.  **Set clear goals and KPIs:** define specific, measurable objectives for your localization and multilingual SEO work.
2.  **Set up proper tracking:** make sure your analytics tools are configured to track performance across different languages and regions.
3.  **Conduct regular audits:** periodically review your localized content and [SEO strategies](https://mikebastin.com/how-ai-is-revolutionising-seo-strategies/) to identify areas for improvement.
4.  **Invest in quality translations:** high-quality, culturally appropriate translations are crucial for a strong return.
5.  **Optimize for local search intent:** [research and target keywords](/services/multilingual-seo/) that reflect local search behaviour and preferences.
6.  **Use hreflang tags:** set up hreflang correctly to help search engines serve the right content to the right audience.
7.  **Monitor competitor performance:** keep an eye on how your competitors perform in local markets to identify opportunities and threats.

## Where to start

[Website localization](https://mikebastin.com/services/website-localisation/) and [multilingual SEO](https://mikebastin.com/services/multilingual-seo/) can deliver significant returns when done well. ROI measurement is an ongoing process. Keep monitoring performance, adapt your strategies and stay attuned to the needs of your international audience to secure long-term success in global markets.
