Stepping off a plane into the warm, orange-scented air of the Turia capital is a moment you’ll never forget. Whether you are moving here to start a new job, retiring under the Mediterranean sun, or joining the growing community of digital nomads, the first few hours can feel like a whirlwind of logistics. Our **Valencia airport guide** is designed to be your “boots on the ground” companion, taking you from the arrivals gate to your first café con leche in the city centre without the typical expat headaches.

We’ve been in your shoes, juggling heavy suitcases, trying to decipher Spanish transport signs, and wondering if we’m being overcharged for a taxi. The good news? Valencia is one of the most navigable and friendly cities in Europe. By the time you finish this guide, you’ll have a clear roadmap for your first seven days, ensuring you spend less time worrying about paperwork and more time enjoying the city’s stunning architecture and lively culture.

## Pre-Arrival: Setting the Stage for Success

Before you even pack your bags, a little bit of preparation goes a long way. Valencia Airport (VLC), also known as Manises Airport, is well-connected to major UK and European hubs. If you’re flying from the UK, you’ll find frequent direct flights from London (Gatwick, Stansted, and Heathrow), Manchester, Bristol, and Edinburgh via carriers like British Airways, easyJet, Ryanair, and Vueling.

### What to Arrange Before You Land

-   **Accommodation:** Ensure you have at least your first two weeks of accommodation booked. If you’re looking for long-term options later, check out this guide on [living in a flat in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/living-in-a-flat-in-valencia-a-pragmatic-overview/) to understand the local market.
-   **Documentation:** Keep physical and digital copies of your passport, flight details, and rental agreement. If you are a non-EU citizen, ensure your visa paperwork is easily accessible.
-   **The “Arrival Package” Option:** Many newcomers choose to book an arrival assistance service. These services often include a private driver waiting at the gate, a pre-activated SIM card, and a “welcome kit” with local maps and essentials. It’s a fantastic way to eliminate the “first-day fog.”

## Valencia Airport Guide: Getting Through the Terminal

Valencia Airport is relatively compact compared to Madrid or Barcelona, which is a blessing. It consists of one main terminal divided into three floors. Arrivals are located on the ground floor (Planta 0).

### The Arrival Process

Once you clear passport control (which can take 20 to 40 minutes during peak summer hours for non-EU passengers) and collect your luggage, you will emerge into the arrivals hall. Here is what you need to do immediately:

### 1\. Getting a Local SIM Card

While the airport has Wi-Fi, having local data is crucial for using Google Maps or calling your host. There is a small shop in the arrivals area that sells SIM cards (usually Orange or Vodafone). Be aware that airport prices can be slightly higher. If you can wait until you reach the city, you’ll find better deals, but for immediate peace of mind, a 20GB “pre-pago” card is a solid investment.

### 2\. Currency Exchange: The Golden Rule

**Avoid the currency exchange desks at the airport if possible.** The rates are rarely in your favour. Instead, use one of the ATMs located near the exit to withdraw a small amount of Euros. Better yet, use a digital bank card like Revolut or Monzo, which offers excellent exchange rates. Understanding [Valencia living expenses](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-living-expenses/) early on will help you budget for these initial costs.

## Getting to the City: Transport Options Compared

You have three main ways to get from Manises to the city centre. Each has its pros and cons depending on your budget and how much luggage you’re hauling.

### Option 1: The Metro (The Efficient Choice)

The Metrovalencia station is located directly under the terminal (Floor -1). Look for the red “M” signs.

-   **Lines:** Line 3 (Red) and Line 5 (Green) both go to the city centre (Xàtiva or Colón stations).
-   **Cost:** Approximately €4.80 for a single ticket, plus €1 for the reusable cardboard card.
-   **Pros:** Fast (20 minutes), frequent, and avoids traffic.
-   **Cons:** Can be crowded; tricky if you have three extra-large suitcases.

_Tip: Buy a “SUMA” card if you plan on using the bus and metro frequently during your first week. You can read more about this in our guide to [Valencia public transportation](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-public-transportation/)._

### Option 2: Taxi (The Door-to-Door Choice)

The taxi rank is right outside the arrivals hall. Taxis in Valencia are white and highly regulated.

-   **Cost:** Expect to pay between €20 and €30 to reach the city centre. There is a minimum airport supplement of around €5.40.
-   **Pros:** No heavy lifting; takes you exactly where you need to go.
-   **Cons:** More expensive than the metro; potential for traffic during rush hour.

### Option 3: The Aero-Bus (Line 150)

The budget-friendly bus runs from the airport to the city.

-   **Cost:** Around €1.50.
-   **Pros:** Very cheap.
-   **Cons:** Slower than the metro; limited space for large luggage; doesn’t run as late at night.

## The First 24 Hours: Settling into the Rhythm

Once you’ve dropped your bags at your accommodation, the real adventure begins. The first 24 hours are about grounding yourself.

### Check-in and Essentials

Most Spanish apartments use physical keys, though smart locks are becoming common. Once inside, check the essentials: Does the hot water work? Do you have the Wi-Fi password? If you’re staying in a traditional neighbourhood, you might find the “50 shades of noise” a bit surprising at first. Valencia is a lively city! You can learn more about the local atmosphere in this article about [Valencia’s unique soundscape](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-50-shades-of-noise/).

### Basic Shopping

Head to the nearest **Mercadona** or **Consum**. These are the two primary supermarket chains. Pick up water (most locals prefer bottled or filtered water), some local ham (Jamón), and fresh bread. _Note: Supermarkets are closed on Sundays! If you land on a Sunday afternoon, you’ll need to find a small “Supermercado 24h” or eat out._

### Safety and Jet Lag

Valencia is incredibly safe, but like any tourist-heavy city, be mindful of your phone and wallet in crowded areas like the Plaza del Ayuntamiento. To beat jet lag, stay awake until at least 9:00 PM. Take a walk in the Turia Park, the 9km green lung of the city, to get some sunlight and fresh air.

## The First Week Essentials: Building Your New Life

The honeymoon phase of the first few days will soon transition into the “getting things done” phase. Here is your checklist for week one.

### 1\. Finalising Your SIM and Connectivity

If you didn’t get a SIM at the airport, head to a shop like **Digi**, **Yoigo**, or **Lowi**. They often offer much better monthly rates (around €10, €15 for massive data packages) than the big-name providers. You will need your passport to register any SIM card in Spain.

### 2\. The Paperwork: NIE and Empadronamiento

If you are planning to [live in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/live-in-valencia/) long-term, these two acronyms will become your life.

-   **NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero):** Your tax identification number. You need this for everything from opening a bank account to getting a gym membership.
-   **Empadronamiento (Padrón):** Registering your address with the Ayuntamiento (City Hall). It proves you live in the city and is required for healthcare and school registrations.

Getting appointments (Cita Previa) can be notoriously difficult. A first-week orientation service helps here, they can often handle the booking systems for you.

### 3\. Opening a Bank Account

While digital banks like **Revolut** or **N26** are great for daily spending, you might eventually need a Spanish IBAN for utility bills. **Sabadell** and **BBVA** are generally more expat-friendly, but be prepared for some bureaucracy. Many people find it easier to use a digital-first Spanish bank like **Openbank**.

### 4\. Explore Your Neighbourhood

Each “barrio” has a different personality. Spend an afternoon getting lost in the streets of Ruzafa (the trendy, hipster hub), El Carmen (the historic old town), or El Cabanyal (the old fisherman’s quarter by the beach). Finding your local “Panaderia” (bakery) and “Fruteria” (fruit shop) is the first step to feeling like a local.

## Common First-Week Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Even the most seasoned travellers make mistakes. Here are the top pitfalls to avoid in Valencia:

-   **Eating Lunch at 12:00 PM:** Most kitchens don’t open for lunch until 1:30 PM or 2:00 PM. If you’re hungry early, look for a place serving “Almuerzo”, a mid-morning snack/meal that is a sacred tradition in Valencia.
-   **Assuming Everyone Speaks English:** While many younger people and those in the service industry do, a little Spanish goes a long way. “Hola,” “Gracias,” and “La cuenta, por favor” (the bill, please) will earn you smiles.
-   **Ignoring the Siesta:** Between 2:00 PM and 5:00 PM, many smaller shops close. Use this time to rest or visit larger shopping centres like Aqua or El Saler, which remain open.
-   **Forgetting to Validate Tickets:** If you use the bus, you must tap your card. On the metro, you tap to enter and sometimes to exit. Fines are steep and inspectors are common.

## Quick Wins for Settling in Faster

Want to feel like a Valencian by Friday? Follow these three tips:

### Join Local Communities

Search for “Expats in Valencia” on Facebook or join Meetup.com groups. There are weekly language exchanges (Intercambios) where you can practice Spanish and meet people who have already navigated the hurdles you’re facing.

### Download the Essential Apps

-   **Valenbisi:** The city’s bike-sharing app. Valencia is flat and perfect for cycling.
-   **FreeNow / Cabify:** The local versions of Uber.
-   **EMT Valencia:** For real-time bus schedules.
-   **Glovo:** The go-to app for food and grocery delivery.

### Learn the “Almuerzo” Culture

Go to a local bar around 10:30 AM and order a “Bocadillo” (sandwich), a drink, and a coffee (the full “Gasto”). It’s the cheapest and most authentic way to integrate into the local social fabric. It’s also a great way to observe the city’s pace of life, which is a key part of the [Valencia expat dream](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-expat/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the Valencia airport metro hours?

The metro generally runs from around 5:30 AM to 11:30 PM on weekdays, with extended hours on Friday and Saturday nights (sometimes until 2:00 AM). Always check the official Metrovalencia app for the most up-to-date schedule, especially during holidays like Fallas.

### Can we pay for a taxi with a credit card at Valencia airport?

Yes, almost all taxis in Valencia are required to accept credit and debit cards. It is always polite to ask “Tarjeta?” (Card?) before you start your journey just to be 100% sure the machine is working.

### Is the tap water in Valencia safe to drink?

The tap water in Valencia is perfectly safe to drink and meets all EU standards. It has a very high mineral content (hard water) and a taste that many find unpleasant. Most residents use a Brita filter jug or buy bottled water for drinking, while using tap water for cooking and brushing teeth.

### How much should we tip in Valencia?

Tipping is not mandatory in Spain. In casual bars or for small meals, leaving the small change (a few cents) is common. In nicer restaurants, a tip of 5 to 10% is appreciated if the service was excellent, but it is never expected or added to the bill automatically.

### What is the best way to find a long-term rental after our first week?

Most locals and expats use websites like Idealista or Fotocasa. Be prepared to move quickly, as the market is very competitive. Having your NIE and proof of income ready will give you a significant advantage over other applicants.
