## Why making an effort with basic Spanish in Valencia matters

Valencia mixes futuristic architecture with narrow, ancient streets. Unlike the tourist-heavy hubs of Madrid or Barcelona, where English is widely spoken in almost every service sector, Valencia retains a more local feel.

You can certainly get round the main sights in English, but the city really opens up once you start using the everyday Spanish that locals appreciate. Making an effort with the language is more than a practical necessity: it is a sign of respect that opens doors to a richer experience.

Locals are proud of their city. Order your _café con leche_ in Spanish and you move from “tourist” to “neighbour”, which often brings warmer service and tips on the best local haunts. Whether you are [living the Valencia expat dream](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-expat/) or just visiting, your attempts will be met with patience.

Hotel staff in _Ciutat Vella_ will speak English, but the vendor at your local _frutería_ in Ruzafa or Benimaclet likely won’t. To handle your admin and feel at home, the basics are what matter most.

## Valencia’s two languages: Castellano and Valenciano

Before the phrases, understand that Valencia is a bilingual city. You will encounter two languages: **Castellano** (standard Spanish) and **Valenciano** (Valencian, the form of Catalan spoken in the region). Castellano is the language most residents use day to day, while Valenciano holds deep cultural weight and co-official status.

You will see Valenciano on street signs, official government documents and public transport announcements. For example, the famous “North Station” is labelled _Estació del Nord_ in Valenciano and _Estación del Norte_ in Castellano.

If you speak Castellano, everyone will understand you, so put nearly all your energy into it: it is what you will use for banking, healthcare and socialising. Keep a little in reserve for the Valenciano expressions that work like a “secret handshake” and make people smile.

## Essential survival phrases organised by situation

The goal isn’t perfection; it’s communication. Here are the most useful phrases by situation, with a pronunciation guide for each.

### Greetings and politeness

In Valencia, it is customary to greet everyone when entering a small shop or a lift. A simple “Hola” goes a long way, but varying your greetings based on the time of day is even better.

-   **Hola** (OH-la): Hello
-   **Buenos días** (BWAY-nos DEE-as): Good morning
-   **Buenas tardes** (BWAY-nas TAR-des): Good afternoon/evening
-   **Buenas noches** (BWAY-nas NO-ches): Good night
-   **Por favor** (por fa-VOR): Please
-   **Gracias** (GRA-thyas): Thank you
-   **De nada** (de NA-da): You’re welcome
-   **¿Cómo estás?** (KOH-mo es-TAS): How are you?
-   **Mucho gusto** (MOO-cho GOOS-to): Nice to meet you

### Shopping at the market or boutique

Valencia is famous for its markets, particularly the _Mercado Central_. Shopping here is an interactive experience where you need to ask for what you want.

-   **¿Cuánto cuesta?** (KWAN-to KWES-ta): How much does it cost?
-   **Quería…** (ke-REE-a): the polite way to order, roughly “would like…”
-   **¿Tiene…?** (TYEH-ne): Do you have…?
-   **Es muy caro** (es mwee KA-ro): It’s very expensive
-   **¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?** (PWE-do pa-GAR kon tar-KHE-ta): Is paying by card OK?
-   **Solo estoy mirando** (SO-lo es-TOY mi-RAN-do): Just looking

### Dining out: restaurants and cafés

Eating is a social ritual in Valencia. Knowing how to handle a menu and the bill makes a real difference. If you find the city’s pace a bit different than expected, remember that [Valencia is a Mediterranean paradise](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-the-not-so-perfect-mediterranean-paradise/) with its own unique rhythms.

-   **Una mesa para dos, por favor** (OO-na ME-sa PA-ra dos): A table for two, please
-   **La cuenta, por favor** (la KWEN-ta por fa-VOR): The bill, please
-   **¿Qué recomienda?** (ke re-ko-MYEN-da): What do you recommend?
-   **Soy alérgico/a a…** (soy a-LER-khi-ko/ka a): Allergic to… (telling staff about an allergy)
-   **Sin carne/pescado** (sin KAR-ne / pes-KA-do): Without meat/fish
-   **¡Buen provecho!** (bwen pro-VE-cho): Enjoy your meal!

### Appointments and admin

Whether you are visiting the doctor or the town hall, these phrases will help you manage your schedule.

-   **Tengo una cita** (TEN-go OO-na THEE-ta): telling reception you have an appointment
-   **Quisiera pedir una cita** (ki-SYE-ra pe-DEER OO-na THEE-ta): asking to book an appointment
-   **¿A qué hora?** (a ke O-ra): At what time?
-   **No entiendo** (no en-TYEN-do): saying you don’t understand
-   **¿Puede repetir, por favor?** (PWE-de re-pe-TEER): Can you repeat, please?

### Banking and housing

When you are [choosing the best neighbourhoods for professionals in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/neighbourhoods-for-professionals-in-valencia/), you will need to talk about flats and finances.

-   **Quiero abrir una cuenta** (KYE-ro ab-REER OO-na KWEN-ta): asking to open an account
-   **Busco un piso para alquilar** (BOOS-ko oon PEE-so PA-ra al-ki-LAR): saying you are looking for a flat to rent
-   **¿Cuánto es el alquiler?** (KWAN-to es el al-ki-LER): How much is the rent?
-   **¿Están incluidos los gastos?** (es-TAN in-klwee-dos los GAS-tos): Are bills included?

## Valenciano phrases worth knowing (the local appreciation multiplier)

If you want to truly impress a local, drop a few Valenciano words into your conversation. It shows you aren’t just learning Spanish, but you are specifically learning _Valencia_.

| Valenciano phrase | Pronunciation | English meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bon dia** | Bon DEE-a | Good morning |
| **Bona vesprada** | BO-na ves-PRA-da | Good afternoon |
| **Gràcies** | GRA-see-es | Thank you |
| **Per favor** | per fa-VOR | Please |
| **Adeu** | a-DEW | Goodbye |
| **Che!** | CHE | Hey! Wow! (a general exclamation) |

_Che_ is the most iconic Valencian expression. It’s used to start sentences, express surprise, or even frustration. “Che, ¡qué bueno!” is a classic way to react to a delicious plate of paella.

## Cultural communication tips: beyond the words

Speaking Spanish in Valencia also means picking up the unwritten rules of how people interact. Communication is high-energy and physical.

### Spanish directness

English speakers often find Spanish very direct. “We were wondering if we could possibly have a coffee, please?” sounds overly complicated here, while a simple “Un café, por favor” is perfectly polite. Brevity is efficiency, not rudeness.

### Personal space and gestures

Expect a smaller “personal bubble”. People stand closer together when talking, and physical touch (a hand on the shoulder or the double cheek kiss for greetings) is common.

Spaniards also speak with their hands, and gestures help when your vocabulary runs out.

### Meal times and the “sobremesa”

Understanding the clock is part of the language. Lunch (_la comida_) is the main meal, usually between 14:00 and 16:00, and dinner (_la cena_) rarely happens before 21:00.

One of the most important cultural concepts is the **sobremesa**, the time spent chatting at the table long after the meal has finished. Never rush away the moment the bill is paid; that’s when the best conversations happen.

### The volume level

Valencia is a loud, lively city. Whether it’s the mascletà firecrackers or just a lively bar, the volume of conversation is higher than in Northern Europe. Don’t be intimidated; the “noise” is usually just a sign of people enjoying life.

## Best resources for learning basic Spanish in Valencia

Combining digital tools with real-world practice is the fastest way to progress.

-   **Language apps:** Duolingo and Wlingua are great for vocabulary, but **Babbel** or **Busuu** tend to offer better grammar explanations for European Spanish.
-   **Language schools:** Valencia has excellent schools like _Hispania_, _AIP Language Institute_, and _Españolé_. These schools offer intensive courses that are great for building a foundation. The [Valencia language schools directory](https://valenciamove.com/spanish-language-schools-valencia/) lists more options by neighbourhood.
-   **Intercambios (language exchanges):** These are the most fun way to learn. Bars like _Big Ben_ in Plaza Honduras (Monday and Thursday evenings, from 20:30, at the time of writing) or various cafés in Ruzafa host weekly exchange nights. You spend 30 minutes speaking English with a local and 30 minutes speaking Spanish.
-   **Immersion tips:** Change your phone’s language to Spanish. Watch Spanish Netflix shows with Spanish subtitles. Listen to local radio stations like _Radio Valencia (Cadena SER)_ to get used to the accent.

-   **1 to 4 weeks (survival):** you can order food, greet people and handle very basic transactions, still leaning on gestures and Google Translate.
-   **3 months (functional):** with consistent study (3 to 5 hours a week), you can talk simply about your life and work, and handle most admin with patience.
-   **6 to 12 months (conversational):** you can follow group conversations (though maybe not every joke) and express your opinions.

The key is **consistency over intensity**: fifteen minutes every day beats a three-hour cram session once a week.

## Common mistakes English speakers make and how to avoid them

Mistakes are stepping stones to fluency, but knowing a few pitfalls saves some awkward moments. Start with the false friends (_falsos amigos_), words that look English but are not:

| Spanish word | Looks like | Actually means | Say instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Embarazada | embarrassed | pregnant | avergonzado/a |
| Constipado | constipated | having a cold | estreñido/a |
| Librería | library | bookshop | biblioteca (library) |

**Overusing “yo”.** In Spanish, the verb ending already tells you who is acting, so “Yo quiero” where “Quiero” will do sounds a bit robotic. Drop the “yo” once you feel comfortable.

**Mixing up ser and estar.** Both mean “to be”: **ser** for permanent traits, **estar** for temporary states. _“Soy cansado”_ says you are tiresome by nature; _“Estoy cansado”_ says you are tired right now.

**Fear of the rolled “r”.** Even if you can’t roll it perfectly, pronouncing it clearly helps locals understand you. Practise “perro” (dog) against “pero” (but).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it possible to live in Valencia only speaking English?

Technically yes, especially in the city centre, but admin, healthcare and friendships with locals all get harder. Even a little Spanish makes a real difference.

### Do you need to learn Valenciano to live there?

No, you do not need to be fluent in Valenciano. Everyone speaks Castellano (Spanish), and learning a few greetings and reading signs in Valenciano is appreciated by locals and helps you find your way around.

### What is the best way to find a language exchange in Valencia?

The best way is to use apps like Meetup.com or search for “Intercambio de idiomas Valencia” on Facebook. There are events almost every night of the week in neighbourhoods like Ruzafa, El Carmen, and near the universities.

### Are people in Valencia patient with Spanish learners?

Generally, yes! Valencians are very proud of their city and usually delighted when foreigners try to speak their language. They are much more likely to help you find the right word than to be annoyed by your mistakes.

### Should you take classes before you move or wait until you arrive?

Some preparation before you arrive, with an app or online lessons, reduces the stress of the first weeks. Once you are here, a local school or tutor helps you apply it.
