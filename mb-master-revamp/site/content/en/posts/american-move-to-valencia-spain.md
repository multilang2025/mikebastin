## Can an American move to Valencia, Spain in 2026?

Yes. Americans are moving to Spain in record numbers, and Spain's immigration system, the **Extranjería**, rewards preparation and punishes guesswork.

In 2026 there are two main residency paths for US citizens: the **Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)** for retirees and passive income earners, and the **Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)** for remote workers. The Golden Visa was abolished on 3 April 2025. The [Spain Non-Lucrative Visa guide](https://valenciamove.com/non-lucrative-visa-spain/) on Valencia Move breaks down the paperwork in more detail.

Below: both paths compared, the 2026 thresholds, the timeline, and the mistakes we see most often.

> Spain issued 15,638 first-time residency permits to US nationals in 2024, against 12,809 in 2023. According to Spain's Immigration Observatory, 48,713 US nationals were resident in Spain on 30 June 2025.
>
> Source: [The Local Spain, "Revealed: record number of Americans move to Spain", September 2025](https://www.thelocal.es/20250924/revealed-record-number-of-americans-move-to-spain)

## NLV or DNV: which path fits you?

Most Americans only need this table to know which path applies to them.

| | Non-Lucrative Visa | Digital Nomad Visa |
|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Retirees, passive income | Remote employees, freelancers |
| Income test (single, 2026) | €2,400 a month (400% of IPREM) | €2,849 a month (200% of SMI) |
| Work allowed | None, anywhere | Remote work; Spanish clients up to 20% |
| Consulate decision period | 3 months | 10 days |
| First authorisation | 1 year | 1-year visa, or up to 3 years from inside Spain |
| Renewal | 2-year periods | To complete 5 years |
| Beckham Law tax option | No | Yes |

> The minimum required amount is equivalent to 400% of the IPREM, plus 100% of the IPREM for each family member. The legal period for a decision is 3 months, and the visa is valid for 90 days (requirements page dated 11 February 2026).
>

> The teleworker must show monthly means of 200% of the SMI, plus at least 75% of the SMI for the first family member and 25% for each additional one. Self-employed applicants may work for Spanish clients up to 20% of their total activity, and the consulate's legal decision period is 10 days.

## The Non-Lucrative Visa for retirees and passive income

The NLV is the route for Americans living off pensions, investments, rental income or savings. The defining rule is strict: **you cannot work**, for anyone, including actively managing a US LLC.

### The 400% IPREM threshold

Spain sets the NLV bar with the **IPREM** (Indicador Público de Renta de Efectos Múltiples). It has been frozen at €600 a month since 2023 and, with the 2023 state budget rolled over again, remains unchanged for 2026, so the main applicant needs €28,800 a year plus €7,200 for each family member. [Moving to Spain's IPREM reference](https://movingtospain.com/iprem-spain/) tracks the current value.

Show it through several months of bank statements with stable, regular funds; a one-off balance screenshot will not pass.

### Trade-offs to weigh

-   No work of any kind, anywhere
-   Health insurance from an insurer authorised in Spain, with no co-payments
-   More than 183 days a year in Spain to renew
-   Spanish tax residency on worldwide income

When budgeting, look at the [real cost of living in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-living-expenses/): lower than most US cities, but rent and insurance add up fast.

## The Digital Nomad Visa for remote workers

Introduced through Spain's **Ley de Startups** in 2023, the DNV covers remote employees, freelancers and business owners whose clients or employers are _outside Spain_.

### Income thresholds for 2026

The DNV threshold rose from €2,763 a month in 2025 to €2,849 after Spain's minimum wage (SMI) went up 3.1 percent by royal decree on 18 February 2026, backdated to 1 January. [Citizen Remote's Digital Nomad Visa guide](https://citizenremote.com/visas/spain-digital-nomad-visa/) walks through the same arithmetic.

| Household | NLV a month | NLV a year | DNV a month | DNV a year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main applicant | €2,400 | €28,800 | €2,849 | €34,188 |
| Couple | €3,000 | €36,000 | €3,918 | €47,009 |
| Couple, 1 child | €3,600 | €43,200 | €4,275 | €51,283 |
| Couple, 2 children | €4,200 | €50,400 | €4,632 | €55,557 |

> The SMI for 2026 is €1,221 a month in 14 payments, €17,094 a year, an increase of 3.1 percent on 2025. NLV figures are 400% of the €600 monthly IPREM plus 100% per family member; DNV figures are 200% of the SMI plus 75% for the first family member and 25% for each additional one.
>
> Source: [BOE, Real Decreto 126/2026, de 18 de febrero, salario mínimo interprofesional para 2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2026-3815)

### Eligibility beyond the income bar

-   **Work history:** at least 3 months with your current employer or clients before applying.
-   **Company longevity:** your employer or client companies must have operated for at least 1 year.
-   **Professional standing:** a degree from a recognised university, or at least 3 years of professional experience in your field.
-   **Remote work clause:** your contract must explicitly authorise remote work from outside the country.
-   **Social security:** Spanish Social Security registration or equivalent cover under an international agreement. US W-2 employees often switch to 1099 status and register as _autónomo_.

### The Beckham Law tax advantage

Remote workers who move on the telework visa can opt into the **special regime for displaced workers**, the **Beckham Law**, provided they were not Spanish tax residents in the previous five years. Employment income is taxed at a flat **24 percent up to €600,000** and 47 percent above, instead of the standard progressive scale, and other income only when it is obtained in Spain.

> Tax scale under the special regime: up to €600,000, 24%; from €600,000.01, 47%. Employment income is deemed obtained in Spain; other income is taxed only if obtained in Spain.
>
> Source: [Agencia Tributaria, Manual práctico IRPF 2025, "Contenido del régimen especial"](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/ayuda/manuales-videos-folletos/manuales-practicos/irpf-2025/c02-irpf-cuestiones-generales/sujecion-irpf-aspectos-personales/regimen-fiscal-especial-aplicable-trabajadores-desplazados/regimen-aplicable-trabajadores-desplazados/contenido-regimen-especial.html)

It covers the tax year of the move and the five following years, and you elect it by filing form 149. Confirm with a Spanish tax adviser first.

> The regime applies during the tax period in which the change of residence occurs and the five following tax periods.
>
> Source: [Agencia Tributaria, "Régimen fiscal aplicable a los trabajadores desplazados: régimen especial"](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/irpf/tengo-que-presentar-declaracion/regimen-fiscal-aplicable-trabajadores-desplazados/regimen-especial.html)

## What happened to the Golden Visa

**Spain abolished the Golden Visa programme on 3 April 2025.** It ran from 2013, and its best-known route required a real estate investment of at least €500,000. Existing holders can still renew; no new applications are accepted. [Global Citizen Solutions](https://www.globalcitizensolutions.com/spain-golden-visa-ending/) covers the background.

> On 3 April 2025 the end of residence permits linked to real estate investments of more than €500,000 comes into force.
>
> Source: [La Moncloa, "El 3 de abril entra en vigor el fin de las Golden Visa", 2 April 2025](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/vivienda-agenda-urbana/paginas/2025/020425-fin-golden-visa.aspx)

Otherwise, look at the NLV, the DNV or investor options in **Portugal**, **Greece** or **Malta**. The [Spain Golden Visa update](https://valenciamove.com/golden-visa-spain/) on Valencia Move lays out what replaced it.

### Documents and submission

Gather an FBI background check (issued within the last 6 months, with the federal Hague Apostille from the State Department), a medical certificate (issued within 90 days of applying), health insurance from an insurer authorised in Spain, and apostilled marriage and birth certificates. Everything needs an official Spanish translation, ideally by a Traductor Jurado authorised by the Ministerio de Asuntos Exteriores. Allow one to three months.

Submit the NLV at the Spanish consulate covering your US address. The DNV can go through a consulate, or be filed from inside Spain during your 90-day Schengen stay, where it goes to the UGE-CE.

> The maximum period to resolve is twenty days from electronic submission; if no decision is issued, the authorisation is deemed granted.
>
> Source: [BOE, Ley 14/2013 de apoyo a los emprendedores, article 76, consolidated text](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-10074)

### After you land in Valencia

Apply for your TIE (foreigner identity card) at the Policía Nacional within one month of entry. Around it come the empadronamiento at the Ayuntamiento de Valencia, a Certificado Digital from the FNMT, and Seguridad Social registration if you are on the DNV.

> A Foreigner Identity Card must be applied for within a period of 1 month from the applicant's entry into Spain.
>
> Source: [Consulate General of Spain in Los Angeles, "Non-working (non-lucrative) residence visa", 2026](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/Consulados/losangeles/en/ServiciosConsulares/Paginas/Consular/Visado-de-residencia-no-lucrativa.aspx)

Legal periods are maximums, and a request for more documents stops the clock.

## The four most common rejection reasons

-   **Unstable proof of funds.** Consulates want months of stable, regular income. For the DNV, a contract that does not explicitly authorise remote work from Spain is an automatic refusal.
-   **The wrong health insurance.** The policy must come from an insurer authorised to operate in Spain (Adeslas, Sanitas, Mapfre, DKV and Asisa are the usual names), with no deductible, no co-payment, no waiting period and no coverage limit. Travel insurance and US plans such as Blue Cross do not qualify.
-   **The 90-day Schengen clock.** A DNV filed from inside Spain must go in before your tourist allowance runs out.
-   **Missing apostilles or uncertified translations.** A US notary stamp is not a Hague Apostille, and a bilingual friend is not a Traductor Jurado.

Most Americans still default to Madrid or Barcelona. In our experience, a couple's monthly budget in Valencia, rent included, runs noticeably lower than in Barcelona, Madrid or Palma, for a better quality of life. Rents move fast, so check Idealista or Fotocasa before fixing a budget.

Both visas lead to **long-term residency after 5 years** of continuous, legal residence. No single absence should exceed six months, and absences should not add up to more than 10 months in total. The NLV renews for 2-year periods and, since 20 May 2025, each renewal requires more than 183 days of real residence in the calendar year. The DNV renews to complete the cycle.

> A renewed non-lucrative residence authorisation is valid for two years, and renewal requires real and effective residence in Spain for more than 183 days in the calendar year (article 64). Continuity for long-term residence is not affected by absences of up to six consecutive months, provided they do not add up to more than ten months within the five years (article 176).
>
> Source: [BOE, Real Decreto 1155/2024, Reglamento de Extranjería, consolidated text](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-24099)

At year 5, the long-term authorisation removes the income test and lets you work in any role. At year 10 you can apply for citizenship after passing the DELE A2 language test and the CCSE culture test. Spain has no blanket dual nationality agreement with the US, so naturalisation usually means formally renouncing US citizenship: discuss it with an immigration lawyer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we work for a Spanish company on the DNV?** Up to 20 percent of your total professional activity can be for Spanish clients. Beyond that you need a standard _Cuenta Ajena_ work visa, which has a much higher bar.

**Do we need to speak Spanish to get a visa?** No, though basic Spanish makes daily life far easier.

**Can our family come with us?** Yes. Both visas allow your spouse or partner and dependent children on the application, with extra funds shown for each (see the income table) and apostilled marriage and birth certificates.

**Why pay for professional support?** DIY applications often draw _requerimientos_, requests for more documents that pause your timeline for weeks, and a refusal can mean restarting while the tourist clock runs.

## Where to start

The DNV with the Beckham Law is the most attractive route for working-age remote professionals. The NLV, with its frozen IPREM threshold, remains the cleanest option for retirees. What catches Americans out is not the law but the precision it demands: plan documents three months ahead, use a real Traductor Jurado, and choose an insurer authorised in Spain.
