## How loud Valencia really gets and how locals cope

_(or better, how to lose your hearing with style)_

In Valencia, noise is not a flaw but a local speciality. In Valencian, noise is _soroll_, a word the city embraces with affection. Fun fact: the most famous Valencian painter bears the graceful name of Sorolla, and the city’s high-speed rail station, València Joaquín Sorolla, shares it. Coincidence? We don’t think so.

Mechanical shutters, wild percussion, crying children, jarring scooters: everything adds to the cheerful Mediterranean racket. Below are 31 of our favourite sounds, grouped by where they will find you, plus the rules that are supposed to keep them in check.

## Festivals and fireworks

**Fallas, firecrackers at dawn.** During the [Fallas of Valencia](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/events-valencia/festivities/the-fallas), the city turns March into an amped-up carnival of sound. Giant monuments, the biggest over 20 metres high and packed with satirical figures called _ninots_, are set alight in a joyful apocalypse. Before that comes the _mascletà_, a synchronised explosion of firecrackers at 14:00 sharp (so much for your lunch on the terrace). A mascletà in Town Hall Square burns around 120 kilos of gunpowder. You don’t watch it, you feel it in your organs: “If you didn’t feel it in your ribcage, it wasn’t a true mascletà.”

> 120 kilos of gunpowder make the Plaza del Ajuntament shake in the first mascletà of the Fallas cycle.
>
> Source: [Ajuntament de València, "120 kilos de pólvora hacen vibrar la plaza del Ajuntament en la primera mascletà", 1 March 2022](https://www.valencia.es/-/primera-masclet%C3%A0-1)

![Street parties (everyone is the DJ but no one is sober)](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Nits-de-Vivers-concierto.jpg)

**Street parties, where everyone is the DJ.** Whether it’s the [July Fair](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/events-valencia/festivities/great-valencia-fear), the [Saint Vicente Martir Festival](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/events-valencia/festivities/sant-vicente-martir-festival) or the night of [Saint John](https://www.lovevalencia.com/en/night-of-saint-john-in-valencia) (San Juan), peace and quiet are banned by order of the city council. On San Juan the beach becomes a giant campsite, a vintage DJ spins reggaeton until four in the morning, and the microphone banter makes audience participation compulsory after three glasses of sangria.

![](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/casal-fallero-valencia.jpg)

**The casal fallero, because indoors is for amateurs.** Fallero clubhouses have too many people for too little space, so they spill onto the street on Fridays, Saturdays and any saint vaguely remembered by Google Calendar. They cheer, they yell “CHE” to someone a metre away, and they leave a trail of plastic cups for the Monday cleaners.

**The marathon batucada.** Somewhere along the marathon route, just as optimism dies, the drummers appear, loud enough to wake ancestors and drown out every thought of quitting.

## Traffic and transport

**Mopeds and scooters, two invasive local species.** Valencia without them is like paella without rice. They are everywhere, day and night, even on the pavement, and there is always one roaring beneath your thin-walled flat at three in the morning. Most [urban journeys in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-public-transportation/) for people without a car run on buses, metro and trams, but the two-wheelers make sure you never forget them.

**Main road traffic, the permanent white noise machine.** Avenida del Cid, Blasco Ibáñez or Gran Vía: an unstoppable flow of cars, screeching brakes, angry horns and nervous restarts. Bonus: from 08:00 to 09:00 you can hear at least 27 creative insults through closed windows.

![Buses](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/bus2.jpg)

**EMT buses, always making an entrance.** They brake grumbling, restart with a sigh and growl at every speed bump. Two buses meeting in a narrow street is like watching two whales turn around in a bathtub, and the robotic voice announces “Next stop…” at full volume.

**Trams and local trains.** Hoping for calm on the **Cercanías** or a tram on **line 10**? Every bend brings a metal squeal, every stop a groan of brakes, and an automated voice declares “Sant Isidre” like a forbidden Harry Potter curse.

![Human speakers in the cars (or how to hear everything unwillingly)](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/human-speaker-in-the-car.jpg)

**Human speakers in cars.** Engine running, windows down, speakerphone on, and you learn everything about the uncle from Zaragoza and the cousin from Denia arguing over a sofa sale. “JUST TAKE IT TO THE HOUSE, DAMN IT!” A true public service.

![The damn sirens...](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/SIrens-in-Valencia-1.jpg)

**The sirens.** Firefighters, ambulances, the local police, the national force or the [Guardia Civil](https://www.nspcoe.org/about-us/sponsoring-nations/kingdom-of-spain/guardia-civil/): every service has its own siren, and they love setting them all off at once.

![Car and house alarms wailing away for no reason and going on and on and on...](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/House-and-car-alarms.jpg)

**Alarms that wail for no reason.** Nobody touched anything, and yet a full-blown siren, straight out of a Hollywood disaster film, wakes the whole street while the owner is on a weekend trip to **Gandía**. The rules give you plenty of time to question your housing choices (see the table below).

## Street life

![Terraces and spontaneous karaoke sessions: the perpetual concert](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Imagen-de-terrazas-2.jpg)

**Terraces and spontaneous karaoke.** Fancy a [clóchina](https://blog.visitvalencia.com/en/clochina-season-is-here) in peace? Too bad. A child has been hitting a spoon against a glass for 23 minutes, two Dutch tourists have been singing _Bohemian Rhapsody_ since brunch, a server shouts orders like a town crier and an old radio plays _Cadena 100_. What you call an invasion of privacy, we call a warm and friendly atmosphere.

**Street sellers and their “Water! Beer! Coke!”** You lay down your towel, close your eyes, and there they come, voices carrying across half the beach, the same one every few minutes. Hoping to escape to [Jardins de Viveros](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/what-to-do-valencia/nature-in-valencia/parks-and-gardens-valencia/royal-gardens-viveros)? They work there too, often teamed up with the fake sunglasses seller.

![The kids in turbo mode in the town squares](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/children-in-turbo-mode.jpg)

**Kids in turbo mode.** Coffee in the [Plaza del Patriarca](https://www.valenciavalencia.com/sights-guide/sights-listings/plaza-patriarca.html) or [Plaza Doctor Collado](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g187529-d24829667-Reviews-Plaza_del_Doctor_Collado-Valencia_Province_of_Valencia_Valencian_Community.html)? Welcome to a mini Grand Prix of scooters, footballs and one little voice shouting “Muuuummm!” from a metre away.

**The afilador de cuchillos.** A squeaky flute through a rusty megaphone, then “EL AFILADOOOOOOR!”: it’s the [knives sharpener](https://www.andalucia4you.net/afilador-en.html#:~:text=This%20very%20recognizable%20sound%20belongs,alert%20neighbors%20to%20their%20arrival.) on his bike. Not once have you handed him a knife.

![Gas cylinders falling from the sky](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Bonbonero.jpg)

**Gas cylinders falling from the sky.** The gas man with his **bombonas** arrives in an orange pickup, shouts “¡El gaaaas!” with an operatic voice, and slams the bottles on the concrete as if waking the neighbourhood were part of the job.

![DJ pigeon](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/DJ-pigeon-1024x585.jpg)

**Pigeons who think they’re DJs.** They don’t coo, they proclaim. Some specimens in Plaza Redonda and the [Monforte Gardens](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/what-to-do-valencia/nature-in-valencia/parks-and-gardens-valencia/garden-monforte-lhort-romero) seem to have merged with car horns. At least they don’t ride scooters.

![The chatty dogs of the neighbourhood](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Dogs-of-the-neighbourhood.jpg)

**The chatty dogs.** No need for doorbells here. Footsteps? Barking. Another dog? Collective barking. Falling leaf? Solo barking. You wanted silence? Get a cat.

![Neighbourhood gossip : unlimited volume](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Neighborhood-gossip.jpg)

**Neighbourhood gossip, unlimited volume.** The _abuelas_ lean on their balconies shouting about everyone’s lives, especially those who are absent. If you hear your name, it’s too late.

**The theatre queue.** People talk, loudly, about politics, paella and Paco’s new girlfriend. By the time you reach your seat you’ve already had Act One, Scene Two and a character arc.

## Noise at home

![Your neighbour's home cinema (no cinema but the volume on full blast)](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Neighbours-being-nosy-1.jpg)

**Doors that slam like Fallas firecrackers.** Each block of flats is a rehearsal room for aspiring pyrotechnicians, and the walk home after a night out delivers the finest performances.

**Your neighbour’s home cinema.** He loves [action films](https://www.betranslated.co.uk/blog/movie-dubbing/), videogames and reggaeton, and shares them with you at 15:00, 22:00 or 03:12. You don’t have Netflix, but you know _Fast and Furious 8_ by heart.

![Dancing furniture in the neighbour's flat](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Dancing-furniture-1.jpg)

**Dancing furniture.** The upstairs neighbour seems to redecorate every two days, and on tiled floors you learn to identify each piece of furniture by its scrape.

![](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/reforma-1-1024x585.jpg)

**La reforma, Spain’s second national anthem.** Inherited grandma’s flat? Time to smash her tiles and lay fake wood from Bricomart. Everyone’s doing it, all year long, within the hours set by the city ([sede.valencia.es](https://sede.valencia.es)):

| Noisy works, Sunday to Thursday nights | Banned 22:00 to 08:00 |
| Noisy works, Friday, Saturday and holiday eve nights | Banned 22:00 to 09:30 |
| Building alarms | Sound for 10 minutes at most |
| Car alarms | Car may be towed after 5 minutes of sounding once police note it |
| Glass collection by lorry | Working days only, never 23:00 to 08:00 |

> Works, repairs, renovations and other noisy domestic activities are banned at night, from 22:00 to 08:00 Sunday to Thursday, and from 22:00 to 09:30 the following day on Fridays, Saturdays and the eves of public holidays (article 30). Alarm systems may not sound for more than ten minutes; a vehicle alarm sounding for more than five minutes after officers note it may lead to the vehicle being removed. Glass containers may only be collected on working days outside 23:00 to 08:00.
>
> Source: [Ajuntament de València, Ordenanza municipal de protección contra la contaminación acústica, consolidated text as amended 22 July 2025](https://sede.valencia.es/sede/descarga/doc/DOCUMENT_1_20250008881965)

If you enjoy legal bedtime stories, here’s the regional noise policy: [mediambient.gva.es: Contaminación Acústica](https://mediambient.gva.es/es/web/calidad-ambiental/contaminacion-acustica.-ruido).

## Work, deliveries and bins

**The pneumatic drill, Valencia’s official instrument.** Imagining Valencia without jackhammers is like imagining paella with chorizo: ridiculous. Day and night the streets pulse to shattering concrete. It’s often the case that a new [building site](https://www.betranslated.com/translation-services/technical/) starts up, and your only refuge is the [Turia Garden](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/what-to-see-valencia/turia-gardens), where you will meet a zumba class with a JBL speaker.

**The never-ending construction sites.** Hammers, cement mixers, cranes and shouted instructions from dawn until about 13:30, when everything stops for lunch, just as your Zoom meeting starts.

**Delivery lorries, the neighbourhood’s alarm clocks.** 05:45, a quiet side road, and _BIP-BIP-BIP-BIP_: the reversing lorry, crashing crates and creaking gates. It’s just [Mercadona](https://info.mercadona.es/en/who-we-are) restocking the yoghurt aisle, with the driver on speakerphone.

**The bin lorry and its glass symphony.** A crash, and you think there’s been an accident. No: the glass container is being emptied at 08:00 sharp, the first minute the ordinance allows on a working day, followed by chains, slamming bins and a conversation at full volume.

![Green Igloos](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/green-igloo-valencia-1024x585.jpg)

**The green igloos.** Those cute bottle banks take glass at 14:00, 18:00 and midnight, whenever someone finishes their third Rioja. Nobody seems to have told the late-night recyclers that the neighbours are asleep.

![Shop roller shutters: BOUM, GRRRR, CLACK](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/shop-shutters.jpg)

**Shop shutters: BOUM, GRRRR, CLACK.** Barely eight in the morning and a metal shutter opens with the grace of a small earthquake, announcing that yes, the phone shop is open.

![](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Generated-Image-November-11-2025-4_45PM-1024x585.jpg)

**The pavement, Valencia’s drum kit.** Square tiles, a delivery man’s steel trolley and tourists dragging 23 kg of hard-shell luggage produce a rolling _clack clack clack_ from dawn. Symphonic. Relentless.

## Then, why stay in Valencia?

![Then, why stay in Valencia?](https://mikebastin.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/valencian-flag-1.jpg)

Are we masochists? No (well, perhaps). Despite the permanent racket, Valencia is irresistible: delicious food, nice tans, loud conversations with everyone and a good life. The sunlight’s beautiful, the beach is close and people smile, even when they honk at you.

You get used to it, and you end up loving the clamour, a bit like the cranky uncle who speaks too loud at family dinner. We wouldn’t exchange him for the world.
