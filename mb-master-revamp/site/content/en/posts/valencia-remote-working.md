Valencia, Spain’s third-largest city, has quietly become one of Europe’s most compelling bases for remote workers and digital nomads. It pairs living costs below Madrid or Barcelona with reliable fibre, a busy coworking scene and a growing international community.

Below we cover why the city works for remote professionals, how connectivity and coworking stack up, what things cost in 2026, which neighbourhoods suit which working style, and where to find the community. Whether you freelance, run a startup or work remotely for a company, [Valencia offers a unique](https://mikebastin.com/work-life-balance-in-valencia/) balance that is hard to find elsewhere in Europe.

## Why Valencia appeals to remote workers

### Climate

Valencia averages close to 2,700 hours of sunshine a year and has mild winters, so outdoor activities and productive working days coexist easily. July and August are hot and humid, though, so air conditioning matters for a home office.

> València's standard climate values record an annual mean of 2,696 hours of sunshine and 93.2 clear days (reference period 1981 to 2010).
>
> Source: [AEMET, standard climate values for València](https://www.aemet.es/en/serviciosclimaticos/datosclimatologicos/valoresclimatologicos?l=8416&k=val)

### Location and size

High-speed trains link Valencia to Madrid and Barcelona, and the airport has affordable flights to major European capitals, which matters when you travel for client meetings or conferences. With around 858,000 residents, the city has the culture and professional opportunities of a major city without the crowds of London or Paris, and it is small enough to build a real network.

> València had 858,178 inhabitants on 1 January 2026, according to provisional municipal register data (INE's provisional estimate for the same date is 849,400).
>
> Source: [Ajuntament de València, provisional population 2026](https://www.valencia.es/cas/actualidad/-/content/poblaci%C3%B3n-provisional-val%C3%A8ncia-2026)

### Culture and work-life balance

The [cultural aspect of Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-expat/) cannot be overstated. The city keeps a strong local identity while welcoming international professionals, which suits remote workers who want to integrate rather than live in an expat bubble.

Long lunches, family time and evening social life create natural boundaries between work and personal life, something many remote workers struggle to hold elsewhere. For more on the expat experience, see our guide to [living the Valencia expat dream](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-expat/).

## Connectivity and coworking

Fibre covers most residential and commercial areas, and home plans of 300 Mbps to 1 Gbps are standard from Movistar, Orange, Vodafone and smaller providers, which is ample for video calls and large file transfers. Competition keeps prices reasonable, and most buildings have a choice of provider.

For [workers who prefer not to work from home, Valencia’s](https://mikebastin.com/live-in-valencia/) coworking scene has grown fast. The [Valencia coworking spaces guide](https://valenciamove.com/coworking-spaces-valencia/) on Valencia Move maps out the main options; these are four well-known names.

| Space | Where | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wayco Coworking | Abastos, Cabanyal, Ruzafa, Pizarro (since 2026) | An established network with a strong community |
| Flying Bean Coffee Workspace | Near Joaquín Sorolla station | Specialty coffee with a small creative community |
| International Coworking Valencia | Valencia | Foreign remote workers and digital nomads |
| Vortex Coworking | Vortex Centro and Vortex Playa | Choosing between the centre and the beach day by day |

Most spaces now offer printing, lockers, kitchens and meeting rooms, and some add showers, podcast studios or video-call rooms with proper lighting and audio. Day passes and part-time memberships let you test a space before committing.

The [pricing structure of Valencia’s](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-living-expenses/) coworking spaces is competitive by European standards: from about €80 a month for a few days to €300 or more for a fixed desk.

> Wayco's published monthly rates, before VAT, range from €80 (Flex 5, Abastos) to €450 (Fixed Plus, Pizarro), with a fixed desk at €220 to €400 depending on the centre (checked September 2026).
>
> Source: [Wayco, coworking rates in Valencia](https://wayco.es/en/prices/)

The coworking culture here leans towards community rather than pure productivity. Most spaces run networking events and workshops, and the [Valencia Coworking and Coffee](https://www.meetup.com/valencia-coworking-coffee/) group meets weekly.

## Cost of living for remote workers

Valencia is still good value compared with Barcelona, Madrid, London or Amsterdam, although prices have risen with international demand.

### Housing

Housing is the largest expense, and rents have risen fast. At the August 2026 city average of €16.3 per square metre, a 75 m² two-bedroom flat works out at roughly €1,200 a month, with outer districts cheaper and the old town dearer. Madrid and Barcelona are still dearer per square metre, by about 43% and 24% respectively.

> Asking rents in August 2026: Valencia €16.3/m² a month (up 3.5% year on year), Barcelona €20.2/m² and Madrid €23.3/m², against a Spanish average of €15.1/m².
>
> Source: [Idealista, rental price report, August 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/09/01/911881-el-precio-del-alquiler-sube-un-5-8-en-agosto-y-sigue-por-encima-de-los-15-euros-m2)

[Idealista.es](https://www.idealista.com/en/) and [Habitaclia](https://www.habitaclia.com/) are the main platforms for long-term rentals. For shorter stays, try [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/valencia-spain/stays) or [Spotahome](https://www.spotahome.com/s/valencia).

## Neighbourhoods for remote workers

Each area suits a different working style.

| Neighbourhood | Character | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ruzafa | Creative quarter, specialty coffee, [Wayco Ruzafa](https://wayco.es/coworking-en-valencia/ruzafa/) | Nomads who want community on the doorstep |
| Montolivet | Residential and quieter, near the [Jardín del Turia](https://www.valencia.es/en/web/cve/eng/green-valencia?p_p_id=com_liferay_asset_publisher_web_portlet_AssetPublisherPortlet_INSTANCE_nkgm&p_p_lifecycle=0&p_p_state=normal&p_p_mode=view&p_r_p_resetCur=false&_com_liferay_asset_publisher_web_portlet_AssetPublisherPortlet_INSTANCE_nkgm_redirect=%2Fen%2Fweb%2Fcve&_com_liferay_asset_publisher_web_portlet_AssetPublisherPortlet_INSTANCE_nkgm_delta=20&_com_liferay_asset_publisher_web_portlet_AssetPublisherPortlet_INSTANCE_nkgm_cur=3) | Those who want green space and lower rents |
| El Carmen | Medieval streets, museums and galleries | Culture lovers who can live with some noise |
| Malvarrosa and the beach | Beach and water sports within walking distance | A work-life balance built around the sea |
| Ciutat Vella | Central, everything within walking distance | Minimal commuting |

[El Carmen represents Valencia’s historic heart](https://mikebastin.com/essential-things-to-do-in-cultural-valencia/), and Montolivet’s rents are generally lower than in Ruzafa or El Carmen. For apartment hunting, our guide to [living in a flat in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/living-in-a-flat-in-valencia-a-pragmatic-overview/) has practical advice.

Neighbourhood choice doesn’t limit access, because buses, metro and trams connect all the major areas. The [Valencia Metro](https://www.metrovalencia.es/en/) is reliable, and our guide to [Valencia public transportation](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-public-transportation/) covers the rest.

## The remote work community

Valencia’s remote work community is one of its most valuable assets. The [Valencia Digital Nomads](https://www.facebook.com/groups/822085512017504/) Facebook group is the main online hub, connecting thousands of members, and groups like the [Valencia Coworking and Coffee](https://www.meetup.com/valencia-coworking-coffee/) meetup organise weekly gatherings.

If you run a local business alongside your remote work, our guide to [optimising your website for Valencia-based searches](https://mikebastin.com/optimising-your-website-for-valencia-based-searches/) helps local clients find you.
