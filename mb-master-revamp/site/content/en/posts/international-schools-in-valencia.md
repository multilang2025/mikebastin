## Choosing an international school in Valencia

Choosing an international school in Valencia takes real research, but narrowing it down to the factors that matter makes the process manageable: curriculum, language of teaching, accreditation, cost and the admissions process.

Together, they decide whether a school will actually serve your child. Below we compare the main schools, explain what each curriculum leads to, and set out how admission works. The [Valencia international schools directory](https://valenciamove.com/international-schools-valencia/) compares the options side by side if you want a second view.

## The main international schools compared

Spain has a large international school sector, concentrated in the two biggest cities, and Valencia's share covers British, American, IB, French, German and Montessori routes.

### Accreditation

For the schools themselves, look for membership of recognised bodies such as NABSS or a BSO inspection for British schools, or authorisation to teach the IB Diploma. These are checked externally rather than self-reported.

Two other names come up often, and they apply to Spanish language academies rather than to schools. [Linguaschools](https://www.linguaschools.com/valencia/) in Valencia, for example, is accredited by the [Instituto Cervantes](https://cervantes.org/), and other academies hold [FEDELE](https://fedele.org/) membership. Both are a useful quality marker if a child, or a parent, needs intensive Spanish alongside school.

### The IB and American routes

The IB Diploma is taught in English with options in other languages, and builds independent research and an international outlook. The American High School Diploma runs over four years of high school, offers broad subject choice and Advanced Placement courses that can earn early university credit, and is recognised by US institutions, which simplifies transfers.

### The British and Spanish routes

British schools follow the National Curriculum for England through key stages to GCSEs and A levels, and most combine English teaching with Spanish language and culture. The Spanish Bachillerato is a two-year course with arts or science tracks, taught mainly in Spanish with bilingual options at some schools, and it lines up directly with Spanish university admission.

### Bachibac in public schools

Bachibac is a dual Spanish and French diploma in which about a third of the curriculum is taught in French. Entry needs B1 French or an entrance exam. Beyond the Lycée Français, public high schools in the Comunidad Valenciana offering it include IES Ausiàs March in Manises, IES José Ballester Gozalvo in Valencia, IES Arabista Ribera in Carcaixent, IES Professor Broch i Llop in Vila-real, IES Carrús in Elche and IES Bellaguarda in Altea.

Montessori and Waldorf approaches are also available at a handful of smaller schools.

## International or local school

International schools teach mainly in English, follow globally recognised curricula and suit a child whose Spanish is not yet strong or whose university plans lie abroad. Local schools teach in Spanish and, in this region, Valenciano. They build fluency and belonging fast and cost far less.

| Factor | International schools | Local private schools | Local public schools |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teaching language | English, often plus others | Spanish, Valenciano | Spanish, Valenciano |
| Curriculum | IB, British, American | Spanish | Spanish |
| Environment | International mix | Mostly local | Local immersion |
| Funding | Private fees | Private fees | Free, tax funded |

The deciding question is where you see the family in five years, and how comfortable your children are with learning a new language.

### Support for children settling in

Moving country is unsettling for children, and most international schools offer help: extra lessons in the main teaching language, counselling through the transition, and activities that make local life make sense. Most also have an established group of expat parents, which becomes a network for advice in its own right.

## What schooling costs

Fees vary widely between schools and rise most years, so we do not quote a range here that would be out of date by the next enrolment round. Ask each school for its current fee sheet; [ASV publishes one](https://asvalencia.org/docs/en/asv-fees-23-24.pdf), for example, and the [International Schools Database](https://www.international-schools-database.com/in/valencia-spain/iale-international-school-valencia/fees) collects published fees by school.

When comparing, look beyond tuition:

-   A one-off registration fee, often non-refundable.
-   Books, materials and technology, and a uniform where required.
-   Lunch, which is often compulsory, and the optional bus service.
-   Extracurricular activities and early or late care.
-   External exam fees such as IB or IGCSE, often charged separately.
-   Sibling discounts, which some schools offer, as [British College La Cañada](https://www.britishcollegelacanyada.es/en/information-and-admissions/) does, and scholarships at [some institutions](https://www.expatica.com/es/education/children-education/international-schools-in-spain-101449/).

Budget for yearly increases when planning more than one year ahead.
