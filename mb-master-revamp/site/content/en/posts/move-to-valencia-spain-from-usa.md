## Why Americans are moving to Valencia

More Americans are swapping the high-pressure US lifestyle for Spain, and Spain granted them more first-time residency permits in 2024 than in any year before.

> Spain issued 15,638 first-time residency permits to US nationals in 2024, up from 12,809 in 2023.
>
> Source: [The Local Spain, "Revealed: record number of Americans move to Spain", September 2025](https://www.thelocal.es/20250924/revealed-record-number-of-americans-move-to-spain)

Madrid and Barcelona were the traditional choices, but many now land in Valencia for a balanced, affordable and culturally rich life. When you [move to Valencia Spain from USA](https://valenciamove.com/moving-to-valencia-from-usa/), you change your pace of life as well as your zip code. As one expat, Sarah from Seattle, puts it: _“In the US, we lived to work. In Valencia, we work to live. Our Tuesday evenings are now spent drinking vermouth in a plaza rather than sitting in gridlock traffic.”_

The city pairs big-city amenities with a coastal town's soul: sunshine most of the year, the Turia park running through the centre, good public transport and a cost of living that keeps a high quality of life within reach. Its green spaces earned it the title of European Green Capital for 2024.

> Valencia was European Green Capital 2024; 97% of its inhabitants live within 300 metres of green urban areas.
>
> Source: [European Commission, "European Green Capital Award: Valencia 2024"](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/urban-environment/european-green-capital-award/winning-cities/valencia-2024_en)

## Choosing the right visa

Two visa types cover most Americans, and a third has closed. For the route most remote workers take, the [Spain Digital Nomad Visa guide](https://valenciamove.com/digital-nomad-visa-spain/) on Valencia Move covers eligibility and timelines.

| Visa | Who it suits | Work allowed | Status in 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digital Nomad Visa | Remote workers, freelancers | Remote, for employers or clients mostly outside Spain | Open |
| Non-Lucrative Visa | Retirees, people with savings | None | Open; €28,800 a year for the main applicant |
| Golden Visa | Property investors | Not applicable | Closed since 3 April 2025 |

> The minimum required amount for the non-lucrative visa is equivalent to 400% of the IPREM, plus 100% of the IPREM for each family member.
>
> Source: [Consulate General of Spain in Los Angeles, "Non-working (non-lucrative) residence visa", 2026](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/Consulados/losangeles/en/ServiciosConsulares/Paginas/Consular/Visado-de-residencia-no-lucrativa.aspx)

The DNV, introduced in 2023, lets you live in Spain while working for US-based companies, often with significant tax advantages; see [Valencia for remote workers and digital nomads](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-remote-working/) to check the fit. The IPREM behind the NLV threshold has been frozen at €600 a month since 2023. For what replaced the Golden Visa, see the [Spain Golden Visa update](https://valenciamove.com/golden-visa-spain/) on Valencia Move.

## Taxes and documents

### Financial planning and taxes

Spain taxes residents on worldwide income. The body you will deal with is the **AEAT** (Agencia Estatal de Administración Tributaria), not to be confused with Google's E-E-A-T, as our piece on [the EEAT vs AEAT typo](https://mikebastin.com/eeat-vs-aeat-typo/) explains. Talk to a cross-border tax specialist early.

### Documentation and the apostille

Every official US document (birth and marriage certificates, FBI background checks) must be translated into Spanish, ideally by a sworn translator (_traductor jurado_), and carry an **Apostille of the Hague**. For the FBI check, the consulate wants the federal apostille from the State Department, not a state one. Start gathering documents at least six months ahead.

## Settling in

Once you land at Manises Airport, the first weeks are a whirlwind of appointments.

### Finding a home

Valencia is a city of distinct _barrios_. Ruzafa is trendy and lively (but noisy), while Patraix feels more local and family-oriented. Our guide on [choosing the best neighbourhoods for professionals in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/neighbourhoods-for-professionals-in-valencia/) will help you narrow the search.

### The NIE and TIE

The **NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero)** is your foreigner identification number, needed for a bank account, a phone contract and a lease. Within one month of entering Spain you apply for your **TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero)**, the physical residency card, which requires your _empadronamiento_ (registering your address at the town hall).

### Bank accounts and belongings

Neo-banks are fine for daily spending, but utility direct debits (_domiciliación_) usually need a traditional bank such as CaixaBank, BBVA or Sabadell, and FATCA brings some American-specific paperwork. As for belongings, shipping usually costs more than the items are worth, and Spanish sockets and voltage (230 V) differ from the US. _Travel light, live light._

## Common pitfalls and how to avoid them

-   **Underestimating the bureaucracy.** _“Vuelva usted mañana”_ (come back tomorrow) is real. Leave a buffer before your move-in date.
-   **Ignoring the noise.** Fallas in March, late dining and stone streets make Valencia loud. Visit a street at 22:00 on a Friday before signing, and read [Valencia: 50 shades of noise](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-50-shades-of-noise/).
-   **Not learning the language.** English gets you through tourist areas, not officials, plumbers or landlords. The Comunitat Valenciana has two official languages, Castellano and Valencian; basic Spanish opens doors.
-   **Mismanaging the empadronamiento.** You cannot get your card without registering at an address, and some landlords renting “under the table” refuse. Make sure your contract allows it.

## Embracing the Valencian lifestyle

In Valencia, relationships come first. Your baker will ask about your day and your neighbours will greet you with _“¡Buenas!”_ in the lift. Enjoy the afternoon lull and the _sobremesa_, staying at the table long after the meal to talk.

Professional life is not on hold either. From [B2B trade shows](https://mikebastin.com/b2b-trade-shows-in-valencia/) to a growing community of digital entrepreneurs, the city offers plenty of networking, and a [work-life balance](https://mikebastin.com/work-life-balance-in-valencia/) that can feel impossible in the States.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we move to Valencia without a job?** Yes, on the Non-Lucrative Visa, by proving savings or passive income. The main applicant needs €28,800 a year in 2026 (400 percent of the IPREM).

**How much money do we need?** Factor in visa fees, flights, a 1 to 2 month rental deposit and at least six months of living expenses, then keep a cushion for exchange-rate swings.

**Is Valencia safe for American families?** Generally yes; the usual worry is pickpocketing in crowded tourist areas. Families will find [top-tier international schools](https://mikebastin.com/international-schools-in-valencia/) and a culture that welcomes children.

**Do we need a car?** Rarely in the centre. Valencia has a good [public transportation system](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-public-transportation/) of buses, trams and metro, plus the Valenbisi bike-share.

**Can we buy property as an American?** Yes, with no restrictions on foreign ownership. On a resale property in the Comunitat Valenciana, transfer tax (ITP) is 9% for deeds signed from 1 June 2026, down from 10%, and 11% above €1 million, plus notary, registry and legal fees. Buying no longer grants residency, since the Golden Visa closed in April 2025.

> From 1 June 2026, the general TPO rate for acquisitions of real estate is 9%; the 11% rate is kept where the value exceeds €1,000,000 (Ley 5/2025, de 30 de mayo).
>
> Source: [Generalitat Valenciana, Hisenda, "Novedades tributarias 2026"](https://hisenda.gva.es/es/web/tributos/novetats-tributaries-2026)
