## The reality of shipping to Valencia Spain: what to bring and what to leave behind

Relocating to the Mediterranean coast is a dream for many, but the logistics of **shipping to Valencia Spain** can quickly turn that dream into a bureaucratic headache. Having navigated this process twice, once from the UK and once helping a colleague move from the States, we can tell you that the “pack everything” mentality is often a costly mistake.

Before you book a container, perform a ruthless audit of your belongings. Spain is a modern European hub with IKEA, high-end boutiques, and excellent local furniture makers. If your furniture is “flat-pack” quality, sell it, because the cost of shipping will exceed its replacement value.

If you own antique heirlooms, high-quality solid wood pieces, or specialised professional equipment, shipping is often the only way to keep your standard of living without a large reinvestment. When considering [Valencia living expenses](https://mikebastin.com/valencia-living-expenses/), remember that while daily costs are lower than in London or New York, replacing an entire household of premium goods is an upfront capital hit you might want to avoid.

The rule of thumb: if it’s sentimental or high-value, ship it. If it’s replaceable and bulky, buy it here.

## Comparing your shipping options: FCL, LCL and air freight

The method you choose for **shipping to Valencia Spain** depends on your volume, budget, and patience. The Port of Valencia is one of the busiest in the Mediterranean, so you have plenty of options, but each comes with its own complications.

### Full container load (FCL)

FCL is the “gold standard” for family moves. You rent a 20-foot or 40-foot container exclusively for your goods.

-   **Pros:** Faster transit times, less handling (reducing breakage risk), and you can pack it to the brim.
-   **Cons:** The most expensive option if you don’t fill the space.
-   **Timeline:** several weeks from North America; much shorter by road or sea from the UK, though post-Brexit customs paperwork now applies.

### Less than container load (LCL) or shared container

Your goods are packed into crates (lift vans) and share a container with other people’s shipments.

-   **Pros:** Cost-effective for small apartments or just a few rooms of furniture.
-   **Cons:** Higher risk of damage due to multiple handlings, and longer timelines while the container waits to be fully booked.
-   **Timeline:** usually the slowest of the three options.

### Air freight

Reserved for the “must-haves” like laptops, essential documents, and a few changes of clothes.

-   **Pros:** Very fast, typically days rather than weeks.
-   **Cons:** Prohibitively expensive for furniture. You pay by weight and volume.

### Shipping methods at a glance

| Method | Best for | Speed | Handling risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| FCL (20ft or 40ft) | 2 to 3 bedroom house | Weeks | Low |
| LCL (shared) | Small apartment or a few rooms | Slowest | Moderate |
| Air freight | Essentials and valuables | Days | Low |

### What the options cost

Removal quotes vary with volume, origin port, season and how much door-to-door service you buy, so treat any published figure as a floor rather than a quote. For moves from the United States, one comparison site publishes these port-to-port ranges in US dollars.

> 20ft container to Spain: USD 4,000 to 8,000. 40ft container: USD 6,000 to 12,000. Shared container (LCL) to European destinations: USD 1,500 to 5,000 or more, depending on volume and route. Sea transit to Europe takes roughly 3 to 8 weeks; air freight 2 to 7 days.
>
> Source: [Sirelo, "Moving overseas shipping container costs", updated May 2026](https://sirelo.com/container-shipping/container-shipping-rates/)

Door-to-door service, packing, insurance and Spanish port charges come on top of those ranges. Get at least three written quotes in euros that itemise each line.

## Shipping a car to Valencia: is it worth the trouble?

The question plagues every expat. Our short answer? **Usually, no.** Unless you own a rare classic or a high-end vehicle that is fully paid off, importing a car to Spain is a bureaucratic minefield.

When **shipping to Valencia Spain**, a car isn’t just a piece of cargo; it’s a regulated machine that must meet EU standards. It goes through a process called _homologación_, and if your car was built for the US or UK market, you may need to change the headlights, indicators, and even the glass to meet Spanish specifications.

### The costs of car importation

1.  **Shipping:** priced separately from household goods, so ask for it as its own line on every quote.
2.  **Import duty and VAT:** if you are moving your primary residence, you may qualify for the duty-free transfer of residence regime, but only if you meet strict timelines and have owned and used the car for at least six months.
3.  **Matriculación (registration tax):** based on CO2 emissions, and the top band is steep.
4.  **The ITV (technical inspection):** a rigorous test to ensure the car is roadworthy by Spanish standards.

> Registration tax (IEDMT) rates by WLTP CO2 emissions: up to 120 g/km, 0%; 121 to 159 g/km, 4.75%; 160 to 199 g/km, 9.75%; 200 g/km and above, 14.75%. Regions, Valencia among them, may raise these rates.
>
> Source: [RACE, "Impuesto de matriculación, ¿cómo se calcula?", January 2026](https://www.race.es/impuesto-matriculacion-coche)

**Decision flowchart: should you ship your car?**

-   Is the car worth less than €15,000? **Sell it and buy in Spain.**
-   Is it a US-spec SUV with high emissions? **Sell it (the registration tax alone will hurt).**
-   Is it a classic car or a sentimental heirloom? **Ship it, but hire a professional gestor.**
-   Are you moving from the UK with a right-hand drive? **Sell it. Overtaking on Spanish roads in a right-hand drive car is dangerous and annoying.**

## Furniture and household goods: customs and regulations

When **shipping to Valencia Spain** from outside the EU, your household goods can enter free of import duty and VAT under the “transfer of residence” (_traslado de residencia_) regime. The exemption is not automatic, and the conditions are specific.

> The applicant must have lived outside the EU customs territory for at least 12 consecutive months before the move, and the personal goods must have been in their possession for at least 6 months before it. The goods must be imported within twelve months of establishing normal residence in the EU.
>
> Source: [Agencia Tributaria, "Traslado de residencia. Franquicias y exención de impuestos", 2026](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/no-residentes/exenciones-viajeros-embajadas-otros/traslado-actividad-empresarial-residencia-otros/traslado-residencia-franquicias-exencion-impuestos.html)

### Prohibited and restricted items

Spanish customs are particularly strict about:

-   **Alcohol and tobacco:** Do not pack your wine cellar in your shipping container. It will be flagged, and you will pay heavy excise duties.
-   **Foodstuffs:** Perishable goods are a no-go. Even dry goods can cause delays if customs officers suspect pests.
-   **Weapons and ammunition:** Even “ornamental” swords require specific permits.
-   **New items:** anything you have not owned and used for six months falls outside the exemption and is liable to VAT (IVA). Keep your receipts.

> El tipo general de IVA es el 21%. (The standard VAT rate is 21%.)
>
> Source: [Agencia Tributaria, "Tipos impositivos de IVA", 2026](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/iva/calculo-iva-repercutido-clientes/tipos-impositivos-iva.html)

### Packaging tips for the long haul

The humidity in the hold of a ship and the heat of the Port of Valencia in July can wreak havoc on your belongings. Use professional-grade, double-walled boxes, and wrap all wooden furniture in “export wrap” (a thick, breathable padded plastic).

Avoid using newspaper to wrap delicate items, as the ink can bake onto the surface in the Mediterranean heat.

## Insurance and documentation: the paper trail

The single biggest mistake people make when **shipping to Valencia Spain** is underestimating the paperwork. If one document is missing, your container sits in the port, racking up daily “demurrage” (storage) fees that quickly outweigh whatever you saved by doing it yourself.

### Essential documents checklist

-   **Copy of passport:** All pages, not just the photo page.
-   **NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero):** Your Spanish tax ID. You should ideally have this before the ship docks.
-   **Baja consular or certificate of residence:** Proof from your previous country that you are leaving.
-   **Detailed inventory:** A line-by-line list in Spanish. Don’t write “Kitchen Box”; write “Kitchenware: Pots, pans, and cutlery (Used).”
-   **Valued inventory for insurance:** This is for the insurance company, not customs.

**A note on insurance:** Never rely on the shipping company’s “basic liability.” It usually pays out a small fixed amount per kilo, which is useless if they drop your MacBook. Buy “all risk” marine insurance that covers the full replacement value.

## Receiving your shipment in Valencia: port to front door

Once your container arrives at the Port of Valencia, the real work begins. If you are moving to one of the [best neighbourhoods for professionals in Valencia](https://mikebastin.com/neighbourhoods-for-professionals-in-valencia/), like Eixample or Ruzafa, you face a particular challenge: narrow streets and old buildings.

### Customs clearance

Your shipping agent will submit your documents to Spanish Customs (_Aduanas_), and clearance usually takes a few working days. If you are lucky, they will clear it on the papers alone; if you are unlucky, they will order a physical inspection, which costs extra.

### The “last mile” delivery

Do not assume a 40-foot articulated lorry can drive up to your front door in the city centre. Most Valencia streets require a “shuttle service”, where the goods are transferred from the large container into smaller vans that can navigate the narrow _calles_.

**Pro tip:** apply well in advance to the Ajuntament de València for a permit to occupy the public road (_permiso de ocupación de vía pública_) so you can reserve the street for the moving van. Without one, the police can stop the offloading and fine you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does shipping to Valencia Spain usually take?

From the UK, expect a matter of weeks rather than months. From the USA or Canada, sea freight to Europe typically takes 3 to 8 weeks port to port, and a shared container is slower than a full one. Always allow extra time for customs clearance at the Port of Valencia.

### Can we ship our pet in the same container as our furniture?

Absolutely not. Pets must travel by air, as cargo or in the cabin or hold of a passenger plane. They need their own health certification and microchipping, completed weeks before departure.

### Do we have to pay taxes on our used furniture?

If you have lived outside the EU for at least 12 consecutive months, have owned the items for at least six months, and are moving your normal residence to Valencia, you can apply for the transfer of residence exemption. You must provide proof of your previous residence and your new Spanish residency (or intent to reside).

### What happens if our goods are damaged during the voyage?

Damage in transit is why marine insurance is non-negotiable. With “all risk” cover, document the damage immediately on delivery (take photos before the movers leave) and file a claim within the policy’s window, which is often only a week or two.

### Is it better to ship or buy new appliances in Valencia?

Buy new. US appliances run on 120 V at 60 Hz, while Spain uses 230 V at 50 Hz. Even with transformers, large appliances like dryers and refrigerators often burn out or perform poorly, and Spanish kitchens are often designed for smaller, European-sized appliances.
